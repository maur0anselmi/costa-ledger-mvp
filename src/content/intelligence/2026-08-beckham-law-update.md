The integration of the Digital Nomad Visa with the expanded Special Expat Tax Regime (*Régimen Especial para Trabajadores Desplazados*, widely known as the Beckham Law) has reshaped cross-border capital allocation across Southern Spain. For international founders, corporate executives, and family offices establishing a footprint in Andalucia, the framework offers substantial fiscal optimization—provided structural compliance is audited prior to relocation.

## Beckham Law vs. Standard Fiscal Residency

Under the expanded regime, qualifying remote workers, relocated executives, and corporate directors moving to Spain can opt to be taxed as non-residents. This grants a flat **24% personal income tax rate** (IRPF) on Spanish-sourced employment income up to **€600,000** annually for six consecutive tax years, compared to progressive Andalusian tax brackets that reach up to 47%.

Crucially for high-net-worth individuals (HNWIs), non-resident tax status under the Beckham Law means foreign-sourced dividends, capital gains, and worldwide personal assets remain exempt from Spanish taxation and foreign asset reporting obligations (*Modelo 720*).

## Andalucia's Fiscal Landscape

Andalucia’s regional government maintains a 100% bonification on its regional Wealth Tax (*Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio*). While the Spanish federal government’s temporary Solidarity Tax (*Impuesto Temporal de Solidaridad de las Grandes Fortunas*) targets net worldwide assets above €3 million, individuals qualifying under the Beckham Law are shielded from this levy on their non-Spanish assets. This distinction makes Marbella, Malaga, and Sotogrande significantly more capital-efficient than competing Mediterranean jurisdictions.

## Critical Audit Points & Operational Pitfalls

* **The 80/20 Income Rule:** Digital nomads applying under contract must prove that a minimum of 80% of their professional income originates from entities based outside Spain.
* **Director Ownership Thresholds:** Startup founders and corporate directors can access the regime provided the underlying company carries out active business operations (passive holding entities are excluded).
* **Strict Application Windows:** The formal election for the Beckham Law must be filed with the Spanish Tax Agency (AEAT) within six months of registering with Spanish Social Security or obtaining local tax authorization. Missing this window permanently defaults the taxpayer into the standard progressive regime.
* **US Expat Considerations:** US passport holders must carefully align Beckham Law flat-rate payments with US Foreign Tax Credits (FTC) to prevent double taxation gaps caused by mismatches in local versus US income sourcing rules.

Pre-arrival structuring remains essential. Navigating the intersection of residency visas, corporate substance, and international treaty protection requires coordination before establishing physical presence in Spain.
