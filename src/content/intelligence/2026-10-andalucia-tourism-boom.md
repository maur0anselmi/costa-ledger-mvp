International visitors spent €82.1 billion in Spain during the first seven months of 2026, but the more important signal for Southern Spain is that Andalucía is growing faster than the country as a whole.

Spain received more than 58.1 million international tourists between January and July 2026, up 4.6% year-on-year. Those visitors spent €82.05 billion, an increase of 7.8%. Spending is therefore rising considerably faster than visitor volumes—a potentially more important indicator for destinations competing for higher-value international demand. 

July strengthened that pattern. Spain welcomed 11.5 million foreign visitors, 4.6% more than a year earlier, while international tourist expenditure increased 10.9% to €18.22 billion. Average spending per tourist reached €1,579 and average daily expenditure €218.

## Andalucía is Outperforming

For Costa Ledger readers, the regional numbers matter more than the national headline.

Andalucía received approximately 1.69 million international tourists in July, up 9.5% year-on-year—more than double Spain's overall growth rate. International visitors spent €2.69 billion in Andalucía during the month, a 23.4% increase from July 2025. Average expenditure per tourist reached €1,597. 

Across the first seven months of 2026, Andalucía received just over 9 million international visitors, an increase of 8.2%, and accounted for 15.2% of Spain's international tourism expenditure. 

The implication is important: Southern Spain is not merely participating in Spain's tourism expansion. On the latest official data, it is gaining international demand and expenditure at a faster rate.

## Málaga Reinforces the International Demand Story

Málaga-Costa del Sol Airport handled 3.05 million passengers in July, 6.3% more than the previous year. International traffic reached 2.56 million passengers, rising 7.3%. In the first seven months of 2026, the airport handled 16.27 million travellers. 

International connectivity remains one of the Costa del Sol's strongest structural advantages for second-home owners, seasonal residents, tourism businesses, and globally mobile families.

But increasing accessibility has a second side. More visitors and higher expenditure can support hotels, restaurants, leisure operators, retail, and other destination businesses, while also adding pressure to transport infrastructure, housing, and highly visited coastal locations.

## What This Means for Property and Investment

The data is supportive of the destination, but it is not evidence that every property is a good investment.

Higher-spending international tourism may strengthen demand for hospitality assets, professionally operated accommodation, restaurants, wellness, leisure, and other visitor-facing businesses. It can also strengthen the appeal of locations capable of converting visitors into repeat residents and second-home owners.

However, tourism growth should not be translated mechanically into residential price appreciation or rental returns. Municipal restrictions on tourist accommodation, operating costs, seasonality, local housing politics, and property-specific licensing remain critical. An investor buying an apartment in Málaga, Marbella, or elsewhere on the Costa del Sol still needs to verify whether the intended rental use is legally permitted and economically sustainable.

Spain's tourism story is increasingly about economic value per visitor as well as visitor volume. Andalucía's July performance is particularly notable: tourism expenditure increased 23.4% while arrivals rose 9.5%.

For international buyers and investors, the signal strengthens the case for Southern Spain as a globally connected, high-demand destination—but also reinforces the need to distinguish destination strength from individual asset quality. The most interesting opportunities may ultimately sit beyond conventional holiday apartments: hospitality, branded accommodation, wellness, gastronomy, managed residences, and businesses serving increasingly affluent international demand.
